For End-to-End-encryption to work properly the two "ends", in the case of a messenger that means two parties of a
conversation, need to share they're public keys with each other.This is facilitated through public key infrastructure (PKI).
In a case where a malicious actor is not a third party but the PKI itself, users will need to ensure
they were served the correct public keys. To do this most messenger apps today provide mechanisms like verification numbers or QR codes which serve as a 
cryptographic summary of the two parties public key.
The parties can be compare them out-of-band to ensure everyone was served the same keys.

Sadly these methods of verification are bothersome at best and comletely unreasonable for conversations
with consisting of many users where the amount of verifications required grows binomially.

# What is key transparency

Key transparency is a concept that tries to replace the manual verification the keys through these methods
with an automatic system based on cryptographic proofs.

It was firstly introduced in the 2014 paper [CONIKS](https://eprint.iacr.org/2014/1004.pdf),
which defines the goal of *key consistency*.
